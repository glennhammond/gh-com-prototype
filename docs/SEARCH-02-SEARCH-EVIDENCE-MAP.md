# Glenn Hammond Search Evidence Map v1

**Date:** 24 August 2026  
**Workstream:** Search, Authority & Discoverability 02  
**Model:** Audience → Need / Question → Search behaviour → Glenn evidence → Entry surface → Evidence strength → Professional value → Gap

## Strategic interpretation

Search demand is not the positioning strategy. The map deliberately favours territories where genuine professional need intersects with first-hand evidence and commercial/professional value.

The most important search opportunity is not a single high-volume keyword. It is a coherent cluster around **designing and changing digital learning systems as connected products rather than isolated courses or technical platforms**.

## Audience model

### Primary

- Heads of Learning / L&D leaders responsible for platform, content and operating-model change.
- Learning technology and digital learning leads who need migration, architecture or production decisions to work together.
- Education/training organisations undertaking LMS or professional-learning platform change.
- Digital learning/product leaders who need user experience, content architecture and technical implementation connected.
- Hiring managers/recruiters seeking senior hands-on capability spanning strategy through implementation.

### Secondary

- Product/UX leaders working in learning or education contexts.
- Agencies or consultancies seeking specialist digital-learning/product capability.
- Professional peers looking for evidence of design-system, Storyline/Rise, Moodle or xAPI practice.
- Regulated-learning teams dealing with complex judgement, assessment or compliance contexts.

### Not a strategic acquisition audience

- General learners seeking introductory definitions of learning theories.
- People seeking generic Storyline tips with no connection to systems/product work.
- Broad consumer wellbeing searchers; Wellbeing Studio is professional evidence, not a consumer acquisition channel for glennhammond.com.

## Current SERP/competitor observations

### Learning platform / Moodle migration

The search landscape is dominated by:

- platform vendors explaining their migration process;
- specialist Moodle infrastructure/development businesses;
- large Moodle partners offering migration, integration and hosting;
- agencies combining migration with learning-design services.

This is a useful competitive pattern. Pure Moodle specialists often stop at the platform boundary; some explicitly state that they do not design/write the course content. Glenn's Connect & Learn evidence is strongest where that boundary breaks down: platform architecture, learning architecture and the course estate had to inform each other concurrently.

### Learning product / experience architecture

The language is less standardised. Results fragment across UX, product design, learning experience design and generic agency content. This lowers the value of chasing one exact phrase but increases the value of making the evidence semantically explicit:

- what the user situation was;
- what changed in the product model;
- what decision was made;
- what was implemented;
- what is not yet proven.

THE RECORD already does this unusually well.

### xAPI / learning analytics

The space is relatively mature technically and includes standards bodies, LRS/platform vendors, practitioners and research. Introductory xAPI content is not a defensible primary authority strategy. The opportunity becomes stronger when Glenn can publish first-hand evidence about statement governance, identity, delivery reliability, analysis and decisions made from the data.

### Assessment / judgement

Regulators and VET providers dominate generic Principles of Assessment queries. Glenn should not compete by rewriting regulator guidance. The differentiated contribution is design evidence: how abstract assessment principles and regulatory hierarchy were represented for experienced practitioners making judgement.

## Entry-surface rules derived from this map

1. **Practice** answers “what kind of practice is this?” and routes to evidence.
2. **Project** answers “what was the body of work and why did it matter?”
3. **Record** is the strongest search/direct-entry unit for consequential professional problems and decisions.
4. **Artefact** should index only when it answers an external visitor's question independently; it is not automatically a search landing page.
5. **Retained knowledge** survives only when it has standalone usefulness, currency or authority evidence not adequately represented by a Record.

## Search-writing implications

- Page titles should describe the actual professional problem and project context, not lead with generic service keywords.
- Record openings should make the problem, decision and evidence boundary legible within the first screen for direct entrants.
- Project pages should carry organisation, period, role, disciplines and concise outcome/context in visible HTML.
- Technology names belong where they materially explain the work; they should not replace the professional claim.
- Related-work links should connect conceptual evidence as well as project hierarchy.
